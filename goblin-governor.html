THE ORIGIN STORY · 14 NOVEMBER 2026

I just needed my bloody laptop to work.

Goblin Governor wasn’t a plan to start a software project. It was an attempt to make an expensive laptop usable.

The machine was an Acer Predator Helios Neo 18 AI (PHN18-72), with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti Laptop GPU. Its owner reported crashes during video rendering, AI work and graphics benchmarks—sometimes before a benchmark properly started.

Windows crash diagnostics identified repeated VIDEO_TDR_FAILURE (0x116) errors involving nvlddmkm.sys, NVIDIA’s Windows driver. That identifies where the failure surfaced. It doesn’t establish whether hardware, firmware, the driver or a combination caused it.

From frustration to experimentation

Driver changes, clean installations, Windows troubleshooting and power-setting experiments hadn’t produced a dependable solution. Returning the laptop or seeking warranty service remained options. But another question was worth testing: could controlling its clock behaviour make it reliable enough to use?

We didn’t start this to create software for GitHub.
We needed the bloody laptop to work.

With help from AI coding tools, we built monitoring and clock-control software. A possible connection between crashes and changing operating states motivated the experiment. That connection remains a hypothesis, not a proven diagnosis.

A fixed clock, then room to breathe

The owner reports that a fixed 1,800 MHz request allowed a previously unreliable Unigine Heaven benchmark to complete. That was a breakthrough in the owner’s experience. A saved score or complete benchmark report hasn’t been verified for the public package, so we describe it as an owner-reported result.

Then we added a separate adaptive mode: allow a lower clock at idle while requesting an upper limit under load. The original fixed implementation remains available as a fallback.

What the records show

During a short model-training session, the app recorded acceptance of a 300–2,200 MHz request. Telemetry peaked at 2,115 MHz and 62°C, then returned to 292 MHz at roughly 4.3 W after the workload.

Two saved training summaries record 40.170 seconds for the earlier fixed-1,800 run and 34.767 seconds for the later run. Their recorded training settings and epoch losses match. This is one comparison, not a repeated controlled benchmark: it doesn’t isolate clocks from caching, warm-up or other system conditions.

Why share it?

Other people facing similar symptoms may want something small they can inspect, test and improve. Goblin Governor is free and open source. It has large text, clear selection states, explicit startup confirmation and a separate recovery watchdog. Unsupported controls are reported as unavailable.

An experiment, not a repair certificate.

This isn’t an official NVIDIA or Acer utility, a guaranteed TDR fix, or proof that a hardware or driver fault has been repaired. It is not a replacement for appropriate warranty service. Recovery still depends on Windows and the driver responding.

We built it because the usual troubleshooting hadn’t given this owner a dependable machine. If sharing the work helps someone investigate their own laptop, it will have been worthwhile.

Built out of frustration. Tested through experimentation. Shared so others can try.
